Understand the Starting Position

Before making your first move, learn to read the position, identify your pieces' needs and discover a plan.

Start Learning ↓

1. Think Before You Move

In Chess960, also known as Fischer Random Chess, the pieces on the back rank start in a randomized arrangement. The pawns remain on their usual squares.

This means that you cannot rely on the same opening setups as in standard chess. Instead, you should examine the position in front of you and decide which pieces need attention first.

The key principle

Don't play a move just because it looks familiar. First, understand what your position requires.

3. Your Four-Step Checklist

STEP 01

Check Your Pieces

Which pieces have clear paths? Which bishops or rooks are blocked by other pieces? Identify the pieces that may need help.

STEP 02

Find Useful Pawn Moves

Look for pawn moves that open lines for your bishops or queen. Consider what each move leaves behind or weakens.

STEP 03

Look for Tactical Problems

Check for immediate threats, undefended pieces and possible captures. Never assume a move is safe without checking your opponent's reply.

STEP 04

Choose a First Plan

Decide which piece you want to develop, which squares you want to control and how you might prepare your king's safety.

4. Common Mistakes

Following a memorized opening

A familiar opening move may not fit your randomized position. Evaluate the actual position instead.

Moving pieces without a purpose

Each move should help development, control important squares, improve king safety or respond to a concrete threat.

Ignoring the opponent

Before playing, ask what your opponent could do in response. Your plan must account for their threats too.

5. Test Your Knowledge

Answer all three questions to check what you have learned.

1. What is the best first step in a Chess960 game?
2. Where must the king start in Chess960?
3. What should you consider before moving a pawn?

Lesson Summary

A strong Chess960 player does not need to memorize every starting arrangement. The goal is to understand the position, develop pieces efficiently and choose a plan based on the position's needs.

Practice this four-step checklist in your next game before making your first move.
