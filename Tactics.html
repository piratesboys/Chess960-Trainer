COURSE 03 · INTERMEDIATE

Early Tactics

Spot tactical opportunities, recognize threats and avoid losing material in the opening.

Start Learning ↓

1. What Are Tactics?

Tactics are short sequences of moves that use concrete opportunities to gain an advantage. They can help you win material, deliver checkmate or escape a dangerous position.

In Chess960, the unusual starting arrangements can create unfamiliar relationships between pieces. A bishop may attack along a different diagonal, or a rook may become active earlier than expected.

The key principle

Before choosing a move, check your opponent's threats and look for forcing moves: checks, captures and direct threats.

2. The Three Questions

QUESTION 01

What Is Under Attack?

Check which of your pieces are attacked. Also look for your opponent's pieces that are undefended or protected only once.

QUESTION 02

What Are the Forcing Moves?

Look at checks, captures and threats that require an immediate response. Calculate the opponent's best reply rather than assuming your idea works.

QUESTION 03

What Happens Next?

Calculate at least one or two moves ahead. Ask whether your piece can be captured, whether the target can escape and whether the final position is actually favorable.

3. Forks

A fork happens when one piece attacks two or more enemy pieces at the same time. The opponent may be unable to save every target.

Example: a knight fork

Imagine a knight jumping to a square where it attacks both the enemy king and queen. Because the king must respond to check, the queen may be lost if it cannot escape.

Knights are especially good at forks because they attack squares in an unusual pattern. However, bishops, rooks, queens and even pawns can create double attacks too.

Your training task: Whenever you consider a knight move, check whether it attacks multiple valuable targets.

4. Pins

A pin occurs when a piece cannot move freely because moving it would expose a more valuable piece behind it to attack.

ABSOLUTE PIN

The King Is Behind

A piece is absolutely pinned when moving it would expose its king to check. A legal move cannot leave the king in check.

RELATIVE PIN

A Valuable Piece Is Behind

A piece is relatively pinned when moving it exposes a more valuable piece, such as a queen. It may legally move, but doing so can lose material.

Bishops, rooks and queens can create pins along diagonals, ranks or files. In Chess960, always examine the actual lines created by the starting arrangement and subsequent pawn moves.

5. Undefended Pieces

A hanging piece is a piece that can often be captured without adequate compensation because it is unprotected or insufficiently defended.

Not every undefended piece is automatically a mistake. A piece might be tactically safe, protected by a counterattack or deliberately offered as a sacrifice. Calculate before deciding.

The safety check

Before moving, ask: which of my pieces could my opponent capture next? After my move, will every important piece still be safe?

6. Think Defensively Too

Strong tactical play is not only about finding your own combinations. It also means preventing your opponent's ideas.

Check before every move

Look for checks and captures your opponent could play after your move.

Count attackers and defenders

If several pieces target the same square, calculate whether the exchange is favorable. Counting pieces alone is not enough; their values and tactical possibilities matter too.

Don't grab every pawn

A pawn may look free but could be poisoned: capturing it might allow a fork, expose your king or trap your attacking piece.

7. Your Tactical Routine

STEP 01

Look for Checks

List the legal checks available to you. Check whether any of them leads to a useful follow-up rather than playing a check automatically.

STEP 02

Look for Captures

Check possible captures and calculate the likely recaptures. Do not assume that winning a pawn is worthwhile if it costs a more valuable piece.

STEP 03

Look for Threats

Search for forks, pins, discovered attacks and targets that are difficult to defend.

STEP 04

Verify the Result

Calculate your opponent's strongest reply. Make sure your combination wins something concrete or improves your position.

8. Common Tactical Mistakes

Stopping the calculation too early

An attractive capture might fail because of a recapture or a stronger intermediate move. Always examine the opponent's reply.

Assuming every attack wins material

Your opponent may have a defensive resource. Check whether the target can move, be protected or create a counter-threat.

Forgetting your own king

A tactical idea is not successful if it allows your opponent to deliver a stronger attack against your king.

9. Test Your Knowledge

Answer all five questions to test your tactical awareness.

1. What is a fork?
2. What makes an absolute pin special?
3. What should you do before capturing a seemingly free pawn?
4. Which move types are useful to examine first when looking for tactics?
5. What should you check after finding a tactical idea?

Lesson Summary

Tactical strength comes from spotting opportunities, calculating accurately and checking your opponent's replies. Forks, pins and undefended pieces are useful patterns, but every position still requires calculation.

In your next game, take a moment before each move to look for checks, captures, threats and possible tactical weaknesses.
