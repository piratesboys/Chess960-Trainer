COURSE 06

Analyze Your Games

Learn how to review your Chess960 games, understand your mistakes, and turn every game into an opportunity to improve.

Start Learning

01 — WHY ANALYZE?

Learn from Every Game

Playing games helps you gain experience, but reviewing them helps you understand what you could do better. Instead of focusing only on whether you won or lost, look at the decisions you made during the game.

Chess960 analysis is especially useful because starting positions vary. Reviewing your games helps you discover which plans work, how you handle unusual piece placements, and where you need more practice.

The goal of analysis

Do not try to find a mistake in every move. Focus on important moments, understand why a decision was inaccurate, and identify a practical lesson you can use in your next game.

02 — YOUR FIRST REVIEW

Analyze Before Using an Engine

Start by reviewing the game yourself. This helps you understand your own thought process instead of immediately relying on an engine to provide answers.

Step 1: Review the opening

Look at how you developed your pieces from the unusual starting position. Did you improve your pieces, protect your king, and respond to your opponent's ideas?

Step 2: Find critical moments

Identify moves where the position changed significantly: a tactical opportunity, a missed threat, a major exchange, or a decision about king safety.

Step 3: Think again

At each critical moment, pause and consider what you would play now. Look for alternatives and calculate your opponent's possible replies.

Step 4: Write down your lesson

Record what went wrong, why it happened, and what you want to remember for future games. Keep your notes short and specific.

03 — FIND THE MISTAKE

Understand Different Types of Mistakes

Tactical mistakes

You missed a fork, a pin, an undefended piece, or a forcing move. Review the position and look for checks, captures, and threats you did not consider.

Strategic mistakes

Your plan did not fit the position. Perhaps you ignored a weakness, failed to improve a passive piece, or started an attack before your pieces were ready.

King safety mistakes

You underestimated an attack against your king or delayed dealing with a dangerous threat. Review which defensive options were available.

Time-management mistakes

You spent too much time on an unimportant decision or played too quickly in a critical position. Consider where extra thinking time would have helped most.

04 — USE AN ENGINE

Check Your Ideas with an Engine

After your own review, use a chess engine to check your conclusions. An engine can reveal tactical resources and stronger alternatives that are difficult to find on your own.

A useful analysis routine

1. Review the game without engine assistance.

2. Mark the moves you were unsure about.

3. Open the game in an analysis tool, such as Lichess.

4. Compare your ideas with the engine's suggestions.

5. Try to understand the reason behind each important difference.

05 — CHESS960 REVIEW

Questions to Ask After a Chess960 Game

Because the starting position changes, your review should focus on the decisions you made in that specific position.

Development

Did you find useful squares for your pieces? Were any pieces left inactive for too long?

King Safety

Did you understand your castling options and recognize when your king needed protection?

Planning

Did your plan match the position? Did you adapt when your opponent changed the situation?

Tactics

Did you check forcing moves before committing to your plan? Did you notice your opponent's threats?

06 — KEEP IMPROVING

Create a Personal Training Plan

You do not need to review every move of every game in great detail. Choose a few important lessons and revisit them regularly.

After each game: Identify one or two critical moments.

After reviewing: Write down your biggest lesson.

During practice: Work on the skill connected to your mistake.

Before your next game: Re-read your previous notes.

For example, if you repeatedly overlook undefended pieces, spend some training time checking which pieces are attacked and which ones are protected before making a move.

07 — KNOWLEDGE CHECK

Test Your Analysis Skills

Answer all five questions to check what you have learned.

1. What should you do before checking a game with an engine?
2. What is a critical moment in a game?
3. What should you do when an engine suggests a move you do not understand?
4. What is a useful result of analyzing a game?
5. Why is reviewing Chess960 games especially useful?

COURSE COMPLETE

Your Chess960 Training Journey Continues

You have completed all six lessons. You have learned how to understand starting positions, develop your pieces, spot tactics, protect your king, build strategic plans, and analyze your games.

The next step is to apply these ideas consistently in your own games. Choose one skill to focus on, review your progress, and keep practicing.
