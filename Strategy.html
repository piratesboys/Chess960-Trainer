COURSE 05

Build a Strategic Plan

Learn how to evaluate a Chess960 position, identify your priorities, and create a practical plan instead of making random moves.

Start Learning

01 — THE BASICS

What Is a Strategic Plan?

In Chess960, the pieces begin on different squares in every starting position. This means that you cannot always rely on familiar opening patterns. You need to understand the position in front of you.

A strategic plan is a series of moves designed to improve your position or exploit a weakness in your opponent's position. It doesn't need to be complicated. A simple plan based on a clear idea is often more useful than several moves without a purpose.

A good plan answers three questions

1. What is happening? Identify the strengths and weaknesses of the position.

2. What do I want to improve? Choose a clear priority.

3. What is my next useful move? Find a move that supports your plan without overlooking threats.

02 — EVALUATION

Evaluate the Position First

Before creating a plan, take a moment to evaluate the position. Look at more than just the material count. In Chess960, piece placement and king safety can be especially important because the starting position may create unusual strengths and weaknesses.

Material

Compare your pieces and pawns with your opponent's. If you are ahead in material, consider whether simplifying the position would help you keep your advantage.

King Safety

Check whether your king is exposed and whether your opponent has threats against it. Consider castling when legal and useful.

Piece Activity

Identify pieces with few useful moves. Look for ways to activate them, improve their squares, or coordinate them with your other pieces.

Pawn Structure

Look for isolated, doubled, or backward pawns, as well as open files and possible pawn breaks. These features can suggest a long-term plan.

03 — FIND YOUR PLAN

Choose One Main Priority

After evaluating the position, choose the most important thing to improve. Trying to solve every problem at once can make your moves unfocused.

Improve your worst-placed piece

If one of your pieces has few useful squares, find a better square for it. An improved piece may help your entire position.

Control an important file or square

Rooks may become powerful on open files. Knights and bishops can be useful when they control important central squares or target weaknesses.

Target a weakness

If your opponent has a pawn or square that is difficult to defend, consider how you can increase the pressure without weakening your own position.

Prepare a pawn break

A pawn break can open lines for your pieces or challenge your opponent's center. Before playing it, check whether the resulting position benefits you.

Remember

Your plan must fit the current position. Do not follow a familiar opening idea simply because it worked in another Chess960 game.

04 — THINK AHEAD

Turn Your Plan into Moves

A strategic idea is only useful when you can turn it into a sequence of practical moves. Before choosing a move, check your opponent's possible replies.

Step 1: Identify the goal

For example, you may want to activate a passive knight or place a rook on an open file.

Step 2: Find candidate moves

Consider two or three moves that could help you reach your goal. Compare their advantages and disadvantages.

Step 3: Check your opponent's reply

Look for checks, captures, direct threats, and tactical ideas that could make your move a mistake.

Step 4: Re-evaluate

After your opponent responds, check whether your original plan still makes sense. Good players adapt when the position changes.

05 — COMMON MISTAKES

Strategic Mistakes to Avoid

Making moves without a purpose

Before moving, ask yourself what the move improves. A move should ideally support a plan, meet a threat, or create a concrete opportunity.

Ignoring immediate threats

A long-term plan is not more important than a direct tactical danger. Always check what your opponent is threatening before continuing with your idea.

Attacking too early

An attack may fail if your pieces are undeveloped or poorly coordinated. Improve your position and bring enough pieces into the attack first.

Refusing to change your plan

Your opponent's moves may change the position. If your original idea no longer works, reassess the board and choose a better plan.

06 — PRACTICE

Your Strategic Thinking Routine

Use this short routine during your next Chess960 game. With practice, it will become a natural part of your decision-making.

1. Check for checks, captures, and threats.

2. Evaluate material, king safety, piece activity, and pawn structure.

3. Identify your opponent's main weakness or threat.

4. Choose one positional goal.

5. Find candidate moves and calculate the opponent's replies.

6. After the move, check whether your plan still works.

07 — KNOWLEDGE CHECK

Test Your Strategic Thinking

Choose one answer for each question, then submit your answers to see your score.

1. What should you do before choosing a strategic plan?
2. One of your pieces has very few useful moves. What is a good strategic idea?
3. Why should you check your opponent's possible replies?
4. What should you do if your original plan no longer works?
5. Which statement best describes a good strategic plan?

NEXT STEP

Learn from Your Games

A strategic plan helps you make decisions during a game. Reviewing your games afterwards helps you understand whether those decisions were effective and what you could improve.
